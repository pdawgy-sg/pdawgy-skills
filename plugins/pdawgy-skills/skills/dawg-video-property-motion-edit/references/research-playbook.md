# Research playbook

Research before planning, so the reel sells what actually makes this property desirable to the
people likely to buy it, and every number on screen holds up. Budget roughly 15–30 minutes. Run
the areas below in parallel where the host allows it (one subagent per area in Claude Code), and
merge the results yourself.

Write everything to `work/brief/research.md`. Give each fact its source URL (or "client
document") and the date you accessed it, and tag it:

- **Verified**: an official or primary source, or the client's documents.
- **Agent's claim**: something he says on camera. It can go on screen as his words; check it isn't misleading.
- **Unverified**: portals, forums, news without a primary source. Use it to get ideas, never as an on-screen fact.

## 1. The project
Developer, tenure, expected TOP, total units, unit mix and sizes, facilities, site plan, launch
status, indicative prices and $psf. Prefer the client's brochure and price list, then the
developer's site, URA and major news. Note anything the client is not allowed to publish. For
example, developers often restrict how co-broke agents may use the project name and branding
before or during a launch, so ask what this agent may show.

## 2. Location
- **Transport:** MRT stations, lines, and the route on foot. Only say "walking distance" or
  "N-min walk" when a routing source such as OneMap backs it. Otherwise use straight-line
  distance (see `style-guide.md`).
- **Schools:** primary schools within 1 km and 1–2 km. Singapore primary registration gives
  distance priority, and families shop by it. Use MOE's school finder or OneMap for distances.
- **Amenities and green space:** malls, hawker centres, parks, park connectors, hospitals.
- **Future plans:** URA Master Plan and announced MRT lines. Say "planned" or "announced",
  with the official date, never "coming soon" as a fact.

## 3. Market and comparables
- Recent nearby transactions and $psf from URA data or portal summaries.
- Check any comparable the agent cites (Amberwood: Thomson Reserve at $3.5M). If his figure
  differs from the record, tell the user. Don't silently edit his words or add a graphic
  that contradicts him.
- Derived numbers ("~27% less") must be plain arithmetic from cited figures.
- Give context, not advice. No yield or capital-gain promises, and no ABSD or financing advice on screen.

## 4. Who buys here
Draft two or three buyer personas from the unit mix, price band and location. Examples: an HDB
upgrader family that wants a top primary school within 1 km, an own-stay couple who want CCR
at an OCR-style price, a parent buying for a child at a nearby university. For each one, write
what they care about and what would make them stop scrolling. The persona the client picks
decides the hook and the order of the selling points.

## 5. What competitors post
Search recent public reels and listings for this project and nearby launches (other agencies,
the developer). Write down their hooks, formats, lengths, graphic styles, music, CTAs, and
which angle everyone already uses. Aim to differentiate rather than copy. Watch public pages
only. Don't download other people's videos unless the client supplies them as references.

## 6. Platform craft
Re-check these each project, because platforms change often:
- **Hook:** the first 1–2 s decide whether people keep watching. Lead with the strongest
  claim, a number or a visual reveal, not a logo.
- **Length:** match it to the platform the client posts on most.
- **Sound off:** many people watch muted, so captions and on-screen numbers must carry the story alone.
- **Safe zones:** app UI covers the top, the bottom (caption text, buttons) and the right
  edge. Conservative guides keep text out of roughly the top 14% (~270 px) and bottom 35%
  (~670 px) of 1920, plus ~65 px each side. Many organic-post guides use a looser centred
  1080×1420 block. Check current guidance for the client's platforms and look at an
  in-app preview. The Amberwood layout puts captions at y≈1560 and the contact lines near the
  bottom, so verify these before assuming they're safe on a new platform.
- **Cover frame:** pick a cover frame with the headline readable in the grid crop.
- **Trending audio:** it can only be added in the app (see SKILL.md step 9).

## 7. Compliance (Singapore)
- CEA's Practice Guidelines on Ethical Advertising (PG 2/2011, v2.0 Dec 2020 at the time of
  writing) apply to social media. They require the identification details in `style-guide.md`,
  ban misleading headlines and claims, and need the owner's or developer's consent to
  advertise. Check cea.gov.sg for the current version at the start of each project.
- Superlatives like "best value buy" are the agent's opinion. Keep them attributed to him (his
  voice, his caption) rather than presenting them as a graphic stating fact.
- Label showflat or artist's-impression footage when the client's materials require it.
- Leads who text the agent go to him directly. Don't put forms or data capture in the video.

## Turn research into angles
End `research.md` with 5–10 **angles**, ranked for the likely persona. Give each angle a
one-line pitch, its evidence (tagged), and a matching moment in the footage or transcript. For
example: "Top-school belt: Methodist Girls' School ~0.7 km (Verified, OneMap) — he says 'near
top schools' at 153111 1.72 s". The angles feed the hook options and the questions in
`creative-brief.md`. Angles with no matching footage or speech become ideas for extra shots
the client could film.

## Grow the skill's knowledge
After delivery, record in `work/brief/lessons.md` what the client accepted, rejected or
changed, and why. When a lesson would apply to future projects, offer to add it to this
skill's `references/` through a pull request on the skill's repository. Never edit an
installed plugin copy.
